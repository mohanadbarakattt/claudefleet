# Digital Product Launch Machine — Playbook

## Before you start: does this machine fit you?

This machine assumes you already have *some* audience or reach — an email list, a following in a niche community, a social presence, or at minimum direct access to people who have the problem your product solves. It is a **distribution amplifier**, not a distribution *creator*. If you have zero existing audience and zero access to your target buyer, building this machine's products will produce well-made products nobody sees — do Claude Money OS's audience-building chapters first, or pair this machine with the Content & Growth Agency machine's distribution engine, before running this one solo.

## The offer

Ship one small digital product per month. Not a 200-page course — a tight, single-problem product a buyer can consume and use in under a week: an ebook/guide, a prompt pack, a template/swipe file, or a mini-course (3-7 short lessons). Price by scope:

- **$9-$19** — single-artifact product: prompt pack, template bundle, checklist system, swipe file.
- **$20-$34** — structured guide or workbook with a clear before/after transformation, or a prompt pack + implementation guide combo.
- **$35-$47** — mini-course (video or written) with multiple modules, worksheets, and a completion outcome.

Scope discipline is the point: a product that takes 3-5 focused days to build (with the agents doing the heavy lifting) beats a "someday" course that never ships. Cadence beats scope.

## Who this is for

**Operator (the buyer of this machine):** a creator, freelancer, or solopreneur who has an audience or niche expertise but no repeatable system for turning it into sellable products. They want a monthly output machine, not a one-off launch.

**End buyer (the operator's customer):** someone actively searching for a solution to a narrow, specific problem inside the operator's niche — not a browser. They buy because the product's title and description name their exact problem.

## Revenue model

- Direct sales via Gumroad or LemonSqueezy, one price point per product (no complex tiering to start).
- Revenue = (email list + organic reach) × conversion rate × price. Early launches lean on personal audience/community; later launches compound via cross-promotion into the growing catalog.
- No ad spend assumed. This is an organic-audience + email-list model. If the operator wants paid acquisition later, that's a separate decision — this machine does not depend on it.
- Do not project specific dollar outcomes. Revenue depends entirely on the operator's existing audience size, niche, and offer-market fit — none of which this playbook can know in advance.

## The catalog effect (how launches compound)

A single product launch has a short shelf life — a spike at launch, then a long tail. The machine's real leverage is monthly cadence:

1. **Cross-sell surface area** — each new product's launch emails and posts can reference "if you liked X, get Y" for anyone who bought a previous product. By month 6, a new subscriber sees 6 products, not 1.
2. **Bundle economics** — once 3-4 products exist, bundle them at a discount (e.g., all 4 for the price of 2.5) as a periodic offer. Bundles reactivate past buyers and raise average order value without new content work.
3. **SEO/search compounding** — each product's sales page is a permanent, indexable asset. Twelve pages after a year outrank one.
4. **List compounding** — every launch adds pre-launch signups who didn't buy immediately but remain on the list for the next launch. Non-buyers on launch N are often buyers on launch N+2 or N+3.
5. **Proof compounding** — testimonials and reviews from early products become social proof used in later launch sequences, lowering the trust barrier each time.

The machine is only worth running if products keep shipping on a fixed cadence (monthly is the default; biweekly if the operator has capacity, quarterly is the floor before the catalog effect stalls).

## Validation-before-build (mandatory gate)

Never let product-builder start writing content until researcher has produced a validation memo showing real demand signals. This is the single highest-leverage discipline in the whole machine — most failed digital products fail because they were built on a guess, not evidence.

**Minimum validation bar before greenlighting a build:**
1. At least 2 independent demand signals (see researcher.md for the signal types: forum threads, competitor product with reviews/sales indicators, recurring search/question pattern, community poll).
2. At least 1 existing paid product in the same problem space (proves people already pay for a solution here — a total absence of competitors is a red flag, not a blue ocean).
3. A one-sentence problem statement the operator can say out loud and have a real prospect nod at ("people struggle with X and currently duct-tape it with Y").
4. A gap or angle the new product covers that existing options don't (cheaper, faster, more specific niche, better format, bundled with X).

If the researcher can't produce 2+ signals and 1 comparable paid product within a day of digging, the idea gets shelved, not built. Move to the next idea on the list rather than force it.

## Monthly cycle (default cadence)

Week 1: validate next idea (researcher) while previous product's launch sequence is still running post-launch emails.
Week 2: build product content (product-builder).
Week 3: build launch assets + pre-launch list building (marketer).
Week 4: launch week (5-email sequence + social + optional ManyChat automation) + support-agent handles buyer questions/refunds as they come in.

Then repeat with the next validated idea.

**If researcher returns a NO-GO on week 1** (fewer than 2 demand signals, or no comparable paid product found): do not force a build. Pull the next idea from your backlog and re-run validation immediately — don't let the calendar slip a full month. If the backlog is empty, spend that week on audience/list-building work instead (see "Before you start" above) and treat it as a deliberate skip, not a stall.
