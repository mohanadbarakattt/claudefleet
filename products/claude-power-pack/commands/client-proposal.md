description: Turn discovery-call notes into a polished client proposal document.
---

Discovery notes / call summary to turn into a proposal: $ARGUMENTS

Produce a complete, client-ready proposal from these notes. If the notes are thin on a required
section, do not invent client-specific facts (their revenue, their team size, their competitors'
names) — instead write the section structure with a clear placeholder and flag it at the end.

## Step 1 — Extract the essentials from the notes

Pull out, explicitly:
- The client's stated problem/pain, in their own words if quoted.
- What they've already tried or why the current approach isn't working.
- Any stated goals, deadlines, or success metrics.
- Budget signals, if mentioned (do not invent a number if none was given).
- Any stakeholders or decision-makers named.

If the core problem itself isn't clear from the notes, say so plainly rather than guessing at a
proposal for the wrong problem.

## Step 2 — Structure the proposal

```
# Proposal: <Client Name> — <Project Name>

## The situation
Restate their problem back to them in plain language, showing you understood it — not a generic
"we understand you need X" but specifics pulled from the notes (their words, their numbers,
their timeline).

## What we recommend
The approach, in plain non-jargon language. Lead with the outcome, then explain the approach that
gets there. Break into phases/milestones if the scope warrants it.

## What's included
Concrete deliverables, itemized. Specific enough that "done" is checkable, not vague scope like
"ongoing support."

## What's NOT included
Explicit scope boundary — this prevents scope-creep disputes later and shows rigor.

## Timeline
Phase-by-phase or milestone-by-milestone, using durations/dates if given in the notes, otherwise
relative ("Week 1-2") with a note that exact dates depend on kickoff date.

## Investment
Pricing structure. If a number was given in the notes or previously agreed, use it. If not,
leave a clearly marked placeholder — `[PRICE: needs confirmation]` — never invent a number.

## Why us
2-3 sentences, specific to what this client needs, not generic self-praise. Reference something
concrete from the discovery call if it supports this (a past project, a relevant capability) —
only if genuinely known, otherwise omit rather than pad.

## Next steps
The exact action needed from the client to move forward (sign, reply, schedule a call), and by
when if relevant.
```

## Step 3 — Tone check

- Confident, plain-spoken, and specific — not stuffed with buzzwords ("synergy," "holistic,"
  "leverage" as a verb).
- Written to the client's actual stated priorities from the notes, not a generic template with
  their name swapped in.
- Short paragraphs, scannable — assume a busy decision-maker reads this in under 3 minutes.

## Step 4 — Flag gaps

End with a short list titled `NEEDS CONFIRMATION:` covering anything you had to placeholder
instead of fabricate — pricing, exact dates, deliverable specifics not covered in the notes.
