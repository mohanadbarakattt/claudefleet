# 04 — Your First Three Clients

The offer and delivery system mean nothing without buyers. This chapter is entirely about getting your first three — after that, you have proof, referrals, and momentum, which is a fundamentally different (easier) game than starting cold.

## Three methods, pick based on your situation

### Method 1: The warm list
Before any cold outreach, exhaust your warm network. Most people skip this out of awkwardness and it's the single biggest waste of an easy win.

The script: message everyone who might know a buyer — not just potential buyers themselves. You're not asking for money, you're asking for a connection or a first shot.

*"Hey [name] — I just started offering [one-sentence offer] for [who it's for]. Not asking you to buy, just wondering if anyone comes to mind who might need this, or if you'd be open to being my first client at a discounted rate so I can build a case study. No pressure either way."*

Send this to 20–30 people minimum before concluding your warm list doesn't work. Most people underestimate how many relevant contacts they actually have once they write the list out.

### Method 2: The niche community method
Find 3–5 online communities (Slack groups, Discord servers, subreddits, niche Facebook groups, LinkedIn groups) where your exact buyer already hangs out. Spend a week just being useful — answering questions, commenting genuinely — before you ever mention your offer. Then:

1. Comment helpfully on a relevant post (no pitch).
2. Follow up in DM only if they engaged with your comment or you have a specific reason to reach out.
3. In the DM, reference the specific thing they posted, then offer — don't pitch cold in the group itself, most communities ban it and it reads as spam regardless.

This method is slower to your first client than paid platforms but produces warmer, more loyal relationships because the trust is built before the ask.

### Method 3: Portfolio-piece-first
If you have zero portfolio and zero warm leads, build proof before you sell. Pick a real (or realistic) example in your niche, do the work unpaid as a showcase, and use it as the centerpiece of your outreach. This is the fastest way to overcome "I have no track record" — you manufacture the track record with one deliberate piece of work.

Concretely: pick a business or creator you admire (don't ask permission, just do sample work — e.g., write 3 sample LinkedIn posts in their voice, or a sample review response for a local business you found on Google Maps), then reach out with the sample attached, not described.

## Ten outreach templates

**DM templates**

1. *Cold DM, portfolio-led*: "Hi [name] — I put together a sample [deliverable] for [their business/context] as an example of what I do for [niche]. No obligation, just wanted to share: [link/attached]. If it's useful, happy to talk about doing this ongoing."
2. *Cold DM, direct offer*: "Hi [name] — I help [buyer type] get [result] without [pain point]. I do this using Claude-assisted workflows with a human quality pass, delivered in [timeframe]. Worth a quick chat?"
3. *Warm intro DM*: "[Mutual contact] mentioned you might be dealing with [problem] — I help with exactly that. Mind if I send over what I do?"
4. *Follow-up after no reply (send once, 4–5 days later)*: "Following up in case this got buried — still happy to send that sample over if useful, no pressure either way."

**Email templates**

5. *Cold email, value-first*: Subject: "Quick idea for [their specific pain point]" — "Hi [name], noticed [specific observation about their business/content]. I put together [specific example] that might help — attached. If useful, I do this as an ongoing service for [buyer type]. Open to a 10-minute call this week?"
6. *Referral request email*: "Hi [name], I'm building out [your offer] for [buyer type] and wanted to see if anyone in your network comes to mind who might benefit. Happy to offer them a discounted first month in exchange for feedback."
7. *Re-engagement email (past contact)*: "Hi [name], it's been a while — I've started offering [offer] and thought of you given [reason]. Worth reconnecting?"

**Comment-then-DM templates**

8. *Comment*: "This is a great point about [specific detail from their post] — I've seen the same issue come up a lot with [buyer type]." Then DM: "Enjoyed your post on [topic] — I actually help [buyer type] with exactly that. Mind if I share what I do?"
9. *Comment on a complaint/pain-point post*: "This is such a common bottleneck — curious, have you tried [relevant angle]?" Then DM only if they reply engaging with your comment: "Following up from the thread — I actually built a process for exactly this if you ever want to see it."
10. *Comment on a competitor's client success post*: "Nice results — curious what your process looks like for [specific step]." Then DM if there's genuine back-and-forth, positioning yourself as a peer, not a poacher.

## Objection handling

**"How do I know this isn't just ChatGPT copy-pasted?"**
"Fair question — every deliverable goes through a manual quality pass from me before it reaches you: fact-checking, voice matching, and a rewrite of anything that reads generic. The AI drafts, I'm accountable for what ships. Happy to show you the sample I sent as proof."

**"This seems expensive for what it is."**
"Totally understand — the price reflects [the result/time saved/risk removed], not just the deliverable itself. If it's outside budget right now, the [floor tier] gets you [scaled-down version] at a lower entry point — want me to send details?"

**"I've been burned by a freelancer/agency before."**
"That's exactly why I built in [guarantee — revisions, refund, no-charge-until-approved]. You're not locked in until you've seen the work meet the bar."

**"I need to think about it."**
"Of course — no rush. Is there a specific concern I can address now, or would it help if I sent a quick sample so you're deciding based on actual work rather than a description?"

**"Can you do it cheaper?"**
"I can adjust scope to fit a lower budget — the [floor tier] does [reduced scope] at [lower price]. What I won't do is the full scope at a discount, since that's not sustainable for either of us long-term. Want to see what the floor tier includes?"

Land three clients using any combination of these three methods before moving to chapter 5 — scaling only makes sense once you've proven the offer converts with real humans, not just on paper.
