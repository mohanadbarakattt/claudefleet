# Outreach System — Landing Your Own Clients

This is the meta layer: how you sell this lead-gen service to the agencies,
consultants, and SaaS founders described in playbook.md, using the same
outbound discipline you're selling them.

## Your Own ICP (starting point)

- Small B2B service agencies or consultancies (1-15 people) with no
  dedicated SDR/sales hire
- Independent consultants billing project or retainer work, high enough
  ticket size that a few extra clients a year matters
- Early-stage B2B SaaS founders (pre-Series A, or bootstrapped) who need
  outbound before they can afford to hire one
- Signal to look for: they're currently relying entirely on referrals or
  founder-led sales and have said so publicly (LinkedIn posts, podcast
  interviews, "how we grew" blog posts)

## 4-Email Cold Sequence (to sell THIS service)

**Email 1 — Intro**

Subject: quick one re: {{company}}'s pipeline

Hi {{first_name}},

Noticed {{company}} looks like it's growing mostly through referrals right
now — which is a great problem to have, but it usually means pipeline gets
lumpy the moment referrals slow down.

I run a done-for-you outbound service: we build the lead list, write and
run the cold email sequence, and hand you a weekly report of who's
interested. You just take the calls.

Worth a quick look?

{{your_name}}

**Email 2 — Value/proof**

Subject: re: quick one re: {{company}}'s pipeline

{{first_name}} — following up.

The way this usually works for agencies your size: you tell us who you
sell to, we handle sourcing the list, writing the emails, and managing
replies. No new hire, no new software for you to learn, and you only see
the leads once they've actually responded.

If it's useful, I can put together a small test batch against your
actual ICP so you can see real replies before committing to anything
ongoing — no charge for that first look.

{{your_name}}

**Email 3 — Case-angle / objection reframe**

Subject: the "does this actually work" question

{{first_name}} — I'll say the honest thing most people are thinking:
you can't know an outbound engine works for your specific offer until
you've tested it against your actual market. That's true of any lead-gen
service, mine included, and I'd rather say that up front than promise you
a number I can't back up yet.

That's why I start every new client with a small paid pilot batch instead
of a guarantee — a real test, real replies, and you decide whether to
continue based on what you actually see.

If that's a fair way to evaluate it, happy to scope the pilot this week.

{{your_name}}

**Email 4 — Breakup**

Subject: closing the loop

{{first_name}} — I'll leave it here for now. If outbound becomes a
priority later, happy to pick this back up — just reply whenever.

{{your_name}}

## LinkedIn / Warm-Network Script

For people in your existing network or warm second-degree connections —
shorter and more direct than cold email:

> Hey {{first_name}} — random one but I remember you mentioning
> {{company}} mostly grows through referrals. I've been running a
> done-for-you outbound service (lead sourcing + cold email + reply
> handling) for a couple of agencies like yours. Would it be worth a
> quick call to see if it's a fit, or is outbound not something you're
> looking at right now? No pressure either way.

Keep it conversational, reference something specific and true about them,
and make declining easy — warm-network trust is worth more than one
aggressive pitch.

## Objection Handling

**Objection 1: "How do I know this actually works?"**

Answer honestly — do not offer a guarantee, because you cannot back one
up and a false guarantee will cost you the relationship the moment it
doesn't hold:

> Fair question, and I won't pretend I can guarantee a result before
> we've tested it against your specific market — nobody honest can. What
> I can offer instead is a small paid pilot: a real test batch against
> your ICP, real emails sent, real replies you can see for yourself.
> You decide whether to continue based on actual data, not my promise.

**Objection 2: "We tried outbound before and it didn't work."**

> That's really common, and usually it's one of three things: the list
> wasn't actually targeted, the copy was generic, or there was nobody
> managing replies and follow-up consistently. I'd want to understand
> what specifically didn't work last time before assuming a re-run would
> fix it — can you tell me a bit about what you tried?

Then use their answer to scope a pilot that specifically addresses the
failure mode they describe (tighter ICP, different copy angle, actual
reply management) rather than repeating a generic pitch.

**Objection 3: "This is expensive for what it is / I could do this
myself with a tool."**

> You could, and some people should — if you've got the time to build
> the list, write and test the copy, and manage replies every week, a
> self-serve tool is cheaper. What you're paying for here is that time
> back, plus someone who's doing this across multiple clients and
> iterating the copy and targeting based on what's actually working, not
> guessing from scratch each time.

Do not disparage self-serve tools or competitors by name — just be
honest about the actual trade-off (time and iteration speed vs. cost).

## Pilot-Instead-of-Guarantee Policy

Always default to offering a paid pilot rather than a guarantee when a
prospect pushes on proof. A pilot is honest (you're both taking on real,
bounded risk), testable (real data after a defined period), and protects
you from ever having promised an outcome you can't control. Never agree
to performance-based pricing (e.g. "pay per meeting booked") as your
default model — it puts your revenue at the mercy of variables (their
offer, their market, their own follow-through) you don't control; if a
prospect insists on it, treat it as a separate, carefully scoped
negotiation, not your standard offer.
