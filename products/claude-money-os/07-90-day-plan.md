# 07 — The 90-Day Plan

A plan turns this playbook into a calendar. Follow the weeks in order — don't skip to "scale" before you've actually landed clients, and don't stay in "setup" past week 2 out of perfectionism. Movement beats polish in the first 90 days.

## Weeks 1–2: Setup and offer

**Week 1**
- Complete the pick-one exercise from chapter 1. Commit to a single lane in writing.
- Draft your offer using the equation from chapter 2 (result + timeframe + mechanism + guarantee).
- Set your three-tier pricing (floor/anchor/premium), checking current comparable rates on Upwork/Fiverr or your niche's typical pricing conversations first. **VERIFY.**
- Set up your file/folder system from chapter 3.

**Week 2**
- Build and run your first prompt chain end-to-end on a test case (not a real client yet) — get comfortable with the QC checklist before it matters.
- Build your portfolio piece (Method 3, chapter 4) if you have no existing samples.
- Write your warm-list message and send it to at least 20 contacts.
- Identify 3–5 niche communities where your buyer hangs out and join them.

## Weeks 3–6: First clients

**Week 3**
- Begin active outreach: warm list follow-ups, 5–10 new DMs/emails per day using the templates in chapter 4, and genuine engagement (not pitching) in your niche communities.
- Track every send in your KPI tracker (below) from day one — don't wait until you "have enough data to bother."

**Week 4**
- Continue outreach at the same or higher volume. Follow up once on anyone who hasn't replied after 4–5 days.
- Have objection responses (chapter 4) memorized well enough to use them conversationally, not read off a script.
- Aim for your first paying client by the end of this week or early week 5 — if outreach volume has been consistent and you're at zero replies, revisit your offer's clarity and pricing before blaming the channel.

**Week 5–6**
- Deliver your first client's work using the full prompt chain + QC process. Over-communicate progress (chapter 5's onboarding message keeps this structured).
- Request a testimonial once the first delivery cycle lands well (chapter 6).
- Keep outreach running in parallel — don't stop sending messages just because one client said yes. Aim for 3 total clients by the end of week 6.

## Weeks 7–12: Systemize and scale

**Week 7–8**
- Templatize any deliverable type you've now done 2–3+ times: save the prompt chain, QC checklist, and file structure as a reusable asset (chapter 5).
- Build your first case study from a completed client engagement (chapter 6).
- Start your weekly CEO hour — same day, same time, every week, without exception.

**Week 9–10**
- If you're at capacity, begin building your first Claude Code subagent for the most repetitive part of your delivery (researcher, drafter, or QC role — chapter 5).
- Evaluate whether any offer is ready to productize into a fixed-scope package.
- Re-check comparable market rates and consider a price increase if you're consistently full or your close rate on first asks is very high. **VERIFY.**

**Week 11–12**
- Push outreach volume back up if your pipeline has thinned — a full calendar today does not mean a full calendar in a month.
- Review your 90-day KPI trend (below) and set week 13's targets based on what actually worked, not what you assumed would work at week 1.
- Decide: double down on this lane, or — only with real evidence, not boredom — layer in a second offer or lane.

## Daily non-negotiables

Every business day, regardless of what week you're in:

- Send outreach or follow-ups (volume depends on your stage — 5–10/day in weeks 1–6, lighter once you have a steady pipeline, but never zero).
- Check and respond to all client and prospect messages within the same business day.
- Run the QC checklist on anything before it ships — no exceptions, no "just this once."
- Log the day's numbers into the KPI tracker before you close out. Untracked effort is invisible effort.

Reading the tracker: if outreach is high but replies are low, the message or targeting is the problem, not your offer. If replies are healthy but calls/closes are low, the objection-handling or the offer's price/value framing needs work. If closes are steady but revenue is flat, it's time to revisit pricing (chapter 5) rather than just chasing more volume.

By day 90, you should have: a proven offer, at least 3 completed client engagements, at least one testimonial and case study, a templatized delivery system, and 12 weeks of real KPI data telling you exactly where to focus next — which is a fundamentally different, much stronger position than the guess you started with on day 1.
