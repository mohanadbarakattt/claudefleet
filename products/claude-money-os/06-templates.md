# 06 — Templates

Copy these directly, then customize the bracketed fields. Every field marked [VERIFY] is something you must fill with your own real, current information — never leave a placeholder or a guessed number in a document a client sees.

## Proposal template

```
PROPOSAL: [Offer name] for [Client name]

Prepared by: [Your name] — [date]

THE SITUATION
[1-2 sentences: the specific problem or gap the client described to you, in their words.]

THE OFFER
I'll deliver [result] within [timeframe], using [mechanism — your process],
and [guarantee].

WHAT'S INCLUDED
- [Deliverable 1, with quantity/frequency]
- [Deliverable 2]
- [Deliverable 3]

TIMELINE
- [Milestone 1]: [date]
- [Milestone 2]: [date]
- Final delivery: [date]

INVESTMENT
- Floor: [$X] — [scaled-down scope]
- Anchor (recommended): [$X] — [full scope]
- Premium: [$X] — [full scope + extras]

NEXT STEP
Reply to confirm and I'll send the scope-of-work and first invoice to get started.
```

## Simple scope of work

```
SCOPE OF WORK — [Client name] x [Your business name]

Effective date: [date]
Tier selected: [Floor / Anchor / Premium]

DELIVERABLES
1. [Specific deliverable, quantity, format]
2. [Specific deliverable, quantity, format]

TURNAROUND
[X] business days per deliverable / [X] business days per batch.

REVISIONS
[Number] rounds of revisions included per deliverable. Additional revisions
billed at [$X] per round.

WHAT'S NOT INCLUDED
[Explicitly list adjacent work that is out of scope, to prevent scope creep —
e.g., "Does not include posting/scheduling on client's behalf" or "Does not
include strategy calls beyond the monthly check-in."]

PAYMENT TERMS
[X]% upfront, remainder on delivery / Net [X] on invoice / Monthly retainer
due on the [X] of each month.

CANCELLATION
Either party may cancel with [X] days' written notice. Work delivered up to
the cancellation date is billed in full.

Client signature: ______________  Date: ______
Provider signature: ______________  Date: ______
```

## Invoice line-item wording

Write line items as outcomes, not hours — it reinforces the value frame from chapter 2.

- Good: "LinkedIn content package — 12 posts, [Month] [Year]"
- Good: "Research-to-brief service — 4 briefs delivered, [Month] [Year]"
- Good: "Review response management — [Month] retainer"
- Avoid: "3 hours of writing" (invites hourly-rate comparison, undercuts your pricing frame)

Standard invoice fields: Invoice number, issue date, due date, your business name and [VERIFY: your registered business details/tax ID if applicable], client billing name, line items as above, subtotal, any tax [VERIFY: your local tax obligations — this varies by jurisdiction and this playbook cannot tell you your specific requirement], total due, payment method/link, and payment terms (e.g., "Due within 7 days").

## Client onboarding message

```
Hi [name] — excited to get started. Here's how this will work:

1. INTAKE: Please share [specific inputs needed — brand docs, past examples,
   login access, whatever the offer requires] by [date]. Here's a quick
   checklist: [link/list].
2. FIRST DELIVERY: You'll have your first [deliverable] by [date].
3. FEEDBACK: I'll ask for feedback within [X] hours of each delivery so we
   stay on schedule — a quick thumbs up/down plus notes is plenty.
4. COMMUNICATION: Best way to reach me is [channel]. I'll check in every
   [frequency] with a status update even if there's nothing urgent to report.

Looking forward to this one. Let me know if anything above is unclear.
```

## Testimonial request

Send this after a client has seen at least one full delivery cycle and expressed satisfaction — never before, and never to a client who hasn't actually gotten results yet.

```
Hi [name] — really glad [specific result they got] worked out. If you're open
to it, would you mind sharing a couple of sentences about your experience
working together? Specifically helpful if you can speak to [the result] and
[what made the process easy/different for you] — but honestly, whatever
feels genuine to you is perfect. No pressure at all if now's not a good time.
```

Never write a testimonial for a client and ask them to approve it as if it were their own words — always let them write it themselves, even a rough version. A testimonial in your buyer's actual voice ("wasn't sure this would sound like me but it did"), however short, reads as more credible than a polished one that sounds like copy you wrote.

## Case study skeleton

```
CASE STUDY: [Client name / anonymized descriptor if client prefers privacy]

THE SITUATION
[1-3 sentences: what the client's problem or goal was before working with you.]

THE APPROACH
[2-4 sentences: what you actually did — reference the specific offer and
process, not generic "we used AI."]

THE OUTCOME
[Only include outcomes the client has confirmed and is comfortable sharing —
e.g., "went from X to Y" — never state a number the client hasn't verified
and approved. If no hard metric is available, describe the qualitative
change instead: "client no longer has to draft these manually" is honest
and still valuable.]

CLIENT QUOTE
"[Their actual words from the testimonial, verbatim]"
— [Name, title/business, or anonymized if requested]
```

Keep every case study honest to what actually happened. A single fabricated or exaggerated number in a case study — once discovered by a prospect doing their own diligence — costs you more trust than the case study ever earned you.
