### Prompt 31 — Discovery Call Script

**What it makes you:** A structured discovery call script that qualifies leads and surfaces the info you need to scope and price accurately.

```
You are a senior agency consultant who runs discovery calls that uncover the real problem, budget reality, and decision process — not just surface-level requirements. Build a full discovery call script.

Inputs:
- Service offered: [SERVICE DESCRIPTION]
- Typical client type: [CLIENT TYPE/INDUSTRY]
- Call length: [MINUTES]
- Info you need to scope/price accurately: [LIST — e.g. budget range, timeline, current tools, decision-maker]

Instructions:
1. Write a warm opening (60-90 seconds) that sets the agenda and puts the prospect at ease, framed as "I want to understand your situation before I recommend anything," not a pitch.
2. Write 5-7 diagnostic questions structured to uncover: the real underlying problem (not just the stated request), what they've already tried, what "success" looks like to them in concrete terms, budget range (asked indirectly if needed, e.g. "what have you invested in this before?"), timeline/urgency, and who else is involved in the decision.
3. For each question, add a one-line note on what to listen for in the answer (red flags, buying signals, scope-creep risks).
4. Write 2-3 natural follow-up probes for common vague answers (e.g. if they say "we just need more leads," a probe to get specific).
5. Write the close: how to summarize what you heard back to them (proves you listened), and how to set expectations for next steps (proposal timeline, what happens next) without over-promising on the call.
6. Include a short "disqualification checklist" — 3 signals that this isn't a good-fit client, so you can politely exit rather than force a bad deal.

Output format:
## Call Agenda / Opening Script
## Diagnostic Questions (with listen-for notes)
## Follow-Up Probes
## Closing Script
## Disqualification Checklist
```

**Sell it as:** Not sold directly — this script raises your close rate and prevents scope-creep on every deal that follows.

### Prompt 32 — Proposal Generator

**What it makes you:** A complete, client-ready proposal document from raw discovery notes — turns a call into a signed deal faster.

```
You are an agency proposal writer who structures proposals to make the decision easy and the value obvious, avoiding generic boilerplate. Write a complete proposal.

Inputs:
- Client/company: [CLIENT NAME]
- Service being proposed: [SERVICE DESCRIPTION]
- Notes from discovery call (problem, goals, constraints mentioned): [PASTE DISCOVERY NOTES]
- Deliverables and timeline: [DELIVERABLES + TIMELINE]
- Price/packaging: [PRICE STRUCTURE]
- Your relevant proof (past results, case studies, credentials): [PROOF POINTS]

Instructions:
1. Write an executive summary (3-4 sentences) that reflects the client's specific situation and goals back to them in your own words — proves you understood the call, not a template.
2. Write a "the situation" section restating their problem and its cost/impact if unaddressed, using language and specifics from the discovery notes.
3. Write "the approach" section: your specific plan/methodology to solve it, broken into clear phases with what happens in each.
4. List concrete deliverables with a timeline (weeks/milestones), avoiding vague language like "ongoing support" without specifics.
5. Include a proof section: 1-2 relevant results or case studies most similar to this client's situation.
6. Write the investment/pricing section with clear packaging, and one sentence framing the ROI/value relative to the cost of the problem.
7. Write a simple, low-friction next-steps section (how to approve, what happens after signing) and a scope note clarifying what's NOT included to prevent scope creep.

Output format:
## Executive Summary
## The Situation
## The Approach (phased)
## Deliverables + Timeline
## Proof
## Investment
## Next Steps + Out-of-Scope Note
```

**Sell it as:** This directly supports closing deals — if it helps you win even one proposal, the value can easily exceed what you paid for this vault. No guarantees; the proposal still needs your judgment.

### Prompt 33 — Scope of Work Document

**What it makes you:** A precise SOW that prevents scope creep and protects both sides — a document every serious client expects.

```
You are an agency operations lead who writes scopes of work precise enough to prevent disputes and scope creep, while staying readable for a non-technical client. Write a complete SOW.

Inputs:
- Client: [CLIENT NAME]
- Service/project: [SERVICE/PROJECT DESCRIPTION]
- Agreed deliverables (from proposal or call): [DELIVERABLES LIST]
- Timeline: [TIMELINE/MILESTONES]
- Price and payment terms: [PRICE + PAYMENT SCHEDULE]
- Number of revisions included: [REVISION POLICY]
- Client responsibilities (assets, feedback turnaround, approvals needed): [CLIENT RESPONSIBILITIES]

Instructions:
1. Write a project overview section stating the goal and the specific deliverables in unambiguous, measurable language (e.g. "10 blog posts of 1200-1500 words each" not "blog content").
2. Write a detailed scope section breaking deliverables into phases/milestones with dates, and explicitly list what's included at each stage.
3. Write an "out of scope" section listing common adjacent requests that are NOT included (e.g. paid ad management if the scope is organic content) to prevent assumption-based scope creep.
4. Write the revisions policy: number of rounds included, what happens beyond that (hourly rate or change order process).
5. Write client responsibilities and turnaround expectations (e.g. "client to provide brand assets within 3 business days of kickoff; feedback within 2 business days of each deliverable") and the impact on timeline if these are missed.
6. Write payment terms (schedule, method, late payment policy) and a simple termination/cancellation clause.
7. Keep language clear and professional, not overly legal — flag that a lawyer should review for final contract language.

Output format:
## Project Overview
## Scope & Deliverables (phased)
## Out of Scope
## Revisions Policy
## Client Responsibilities
## Payment Terms
## Termination Clause
Note at the end: "This is a operational SOW template, not legal advice — have a lawyer review before use as a binding contract."
```

**Sell it as:** Protects every project margin you run — prevents the unpaid scope creep that quietly erodes agency profitability.

### Prompt 34 — Client Onboarding Document

**What it makes you:** A polished onboarding packet that makes new clients feel confident and sets you up for a smooth engagement.

```
You are an agency client success lead who designs onboarding experiences that reduce early churn and set clear expectations from day one. Build a complete onboarding document.

Inputs:
- Service/agency: [SERVICE/AGENCY NAME]
- What the client just purchased: [SERVICE PURCHASED]
- Team/point of contact structure: [WHO THE CLIENT WILL WORK WITH]
- Tools/platforms used (project management, communication): [TOOLS]
- What's needed from the client to start (assets, access, info): [INTAKE REQUIREMENTS]
- Typical timeline to first deliverable: [TIMELINE]

Instructions:
1. Write a welcome section that reinforces the client's decision (brief, confidence-building, not salesy) and sets the tone for the relationship.
2. Write "what to expect" section: communication cadence, response time expectations, how updates will be shared, and who to contact for what.
3. Write the intake checklist: exactly what the client needs to provide (access, brand assets, logins, background info) with a due date to avoid delaying kickoff.
4. Write a timeline section: key milestones from kickoff to first deliverable, so the client knows what happens when.
5. Write a "how we work together" section covering feedback process, revision process, and escalation path if something's not working.
6. Include a short FAQ addressing the 5 most common early-stage client questions for this type of service.
7. End with a clear single next action for the client (e.g. "complete this intake form by [date]").

Output format:
## Welcome
## What to Expect
## Intake Checklist (with due date)
## Timeline to First Deliverable
## How We Work Together
## FAQ
## Your Next Step
```

**Sell it as:** Reduces early-stage churn and support questions — a one-time build that pays back on every new client afterward.

### Prompt 35 — Monthly Client Report Writer

**What it makes you:** A polished monthly performance report from raw data — the deliverable that justifies your retainer every single month.

```
You are an agency account manager who writes monthly client reports that highlight real progress, contextualize numbers, and reinforce the value of the retainer. Write the report below.

Inputs:
- Client/service: [CLIENT NAME/SERVICE]
- Reporting period: [MONTH/DATE RANGE]
- Raw metrics/data (paste numbers, however messy): [PASTE RAW DATA]
- Goals set at the start of the engagement: [GOALS]
- Work completed this period (deliverables shipped): [DELIVERABLES LIST]
- Anything that underperformed or was delayed, and why: [CHALLENGES/CONTEXT]

Instructions:
1. Write an executive summary (3-4 sentences) leading with the headline result or biggest win of the period, framed against the original goals.
2. Present the key metrics in a clear table, each with the prior period's number for comparison and a one-line interpretation (not just raw numbers — explain what changed and why it matters).
3. List deliverables completed this period as concrete proof of work done, not just "ongoing management."
4. If anything underperformed, address it directly and non-defensively: state what happened, why, and the specific corrective action being taken — never bury or omit bad news, as trust erodes faster from omission than from honesty.
5. Write a "next month" section: 2-3 specific priorities and what the client should expect.
6. End with a short section reinforcing overall trajectory toward the original goals (are we ahead, on track, or behind — and what that means).

Output format:
## Executive Summary
## Key Metrics (table + interpretation)
## Deliverables Completed
## Challenges & Corrective Actions
## Next Month's Priorities
## Trajectory vs. Goals
```

**Sell it as:** This is the retainer-justification engine — a strong report is the single biggest lever against client churn.

### Prompt 36 — Churn-Save Email

**What it makes you:** A well-crafted retention email for a client showing signs of leaving — directly saves recurring revenue.

```
You are a client retention specialist who writes save-the-account messages that address real concerns honestly rather than using generic retention scripts. Write a churn-save email.

Inputs:
- Client: [CLIENT NAME]
- Signal that they may be leaving (reduced engagement, explicit cancellation notice, complaint): [SIGNAL/CONTEXT]
- Known or suspected reason (price, results, poor communication, priorities changed): [REASON]
- Relationship history/results delivered so far: [HISTORY/RESULTS]
- What you're able to offer to address it (if applicable — price adjustment, scope change, extra attention): [WHAT YOU CAN OFFER]

Instructions:
1. If they've already stated a reason (e.g. an explicit cancellation email), write a direct response that acknowledges their specific concern first, without being defensive or immediately pitching a discount.
2. If the reason is only suspected (e.g. declining engagement with no explanation), write an outreach email that opens the door honestly ("I've noticed X, and wanted to check in") rather than pretending everything's fine.
3. Briefly and factually remind them of real results/value delivered so far — specific, not generic ("in the last 3 months we delivered X, Y, Z") — without sounding defensive.
4. Address the likely reason directly: if it's results, propose a specific plan adjustment; if it's price, address value or offer a reasonable adjustment only if you're prepared to honor it; if it's communication, acknowledge it and commit to a specific fix.
5. End with a low-pressure, concrete next step (a 15-minute call, not "let me know if you have questions") and make clear you'd rather hear the truth than have them quietly churn.
6. Keep the tone human and non-salesy throughout — this is a relationship email, not a sales email.

Output format:
Subject line / Full email body / One-line internal note on the retention lever being used (value reminder, price flex, plan change, communication fix).
```

**Sell it as:** Directly protects MRR — if it helps you save even one retainer client, that saved revenue can far exceed the cost of this vault.

### Prompt 37 — Case Study Builder

**What it makes you:** A polished, specific case study from a completed client engagement — your best sales asset for future proposals.

```
You are a case study writer who builds specific, credible client success stories that read as evidence, not marketing fluff. Write a complete case study.

Inputs:
- Client (or anonymized description if confidential): [CLIENT NAME/DESCRIPTION]
- Starting situation/problem before working together: [STARTING SITUATION]
- What you did (services, approach, key decisions): [WHAT YOU DID]
- Results (metrics, outcomes — as specific as possible): [RESULTS DATA]
- Direct client quotes if available: [QUOTES OR "none yet — flag as needed"]
- Where this case study will be used: [SALES PAGE / PROPOSAL / PORTFOLIO]

Instructions:
1. Write a results-forward headline stating the specific outcome achieved (a number or concrete transformation, not "a successful partnership").
2. Write "the challenge" section: the client's specific starting situation and what was at stake, in concrete terms a prospect in a similar position would recognize themselves in.
3. Write "the approach" section: what you actually did, structured as a short narrative with 2-4 key decisions or turning points, avoiding generic "we developed a strategy" language in favor of specifics.
4. Write "the results" section: lead with the headline metric, then supporting metrics, presented clearly (a short table if multiple metrics).
5. Weave in the client quote(s) at the point in the narrative where they add the most credibility, or flag exactly what question to ask the client if no quote exists yet.
6. Write a short closing section connecting this result back to what a new prospect reading this could expect, without over-promising identical results.

Output format:
## Headline
## The Challenge
## The Approach
## The Results (with metrics table if applicable)
## Client Quote
## Closing (relevance to future prospects)
If no results data was provided, flag exactly which 2-3 numbers to request from the client before publishing.
```

**Sell it as:** Every case study you build shortens future sales cycles — treat this as a required deliverable at the end of every successful engagement.

### Prompt 38 — Referral Ask

**What it makes you:** A natural, non-awkward referral request that actually generates warm introductions from happy clients.

```
You are a client relationship specialist who asks for referrals in a way that feels natural and easy to act on, never awkward or transactional. Write a referral request.

Inputs:
- Client: [CLIENT NAME]
- Relationship context (how long you've worked together, results delivered): [CONTEXT]
- Format (email, message, in-person/call script): [FORMAT]
- Ideal referral profile (who would be a good fit): [IDEAL REFERRAL DESCRIPTION]
- Any incentive being offered (optional): [INCENTIVE OR "none"]

Instructions:
1. Open by referencing a specific, recent positive moment in the relationship (a result delivered, a compliment they gave) as the natural trigger for the ask — never ask cold or generically.
2. Make the ask specific and easy to act on: describe the exact type of person/company that would be a good fit (not "anyone who might need this"), so the client can immediately think of 1-2 names.
3. Make the action low-friction: suggest the easiest possible next step (e.g. "a quick intro email is all it takes" or "feel free to just share my info") rather than asking them to do heavy lifting.
4. If there's an incentive, mention it briefly and without making the ask feel transactional — frame it as a thank-you, not a bribe.
5. Close warmly, making clear there's no pressure and the relationship isn't contingent on saying yes.

Output format:
Subject line (if email) / Full message body, under 120 words, natural and warm in tone.
```

**Sell it as:** Referrals are the highest-margin lead source in any service business — this prompt turns satisfaction into pipeline systematically.

### Prompt 39 — Retainer Pitch (Upsell from Project to Ongoing)

**What it makes you:** A compelling pitch to convert a one-off project client into a recurring retainer — the highest-leverage revenue move in agency work.

```
You are an agency growth lead who converts satisfied project clients into recurring retainer clients by demonstrating ongoing value, not just asking for more money. Write a retainer pitch.

Inputs:
- Client: [CLIENT NAME]
- The project just completed and its results: [PROJECT + RESULTS]
- The ongoing need this reveals (why a one-time project isn't enough): [ONGOING NEED]
- Proposed retainer scope and price: [RETAINER SCOPE + PRICE]
- Format (email, proposal doc, call script): [FORMAT]

Instructions:
1. Open by referencing the specific results from the completed project as proof of capability — the retainer pitch should feel like a natural continuation, not a new sales pitch.
2. Articulate the specific ongoing problem that a one-time project doesn't solve (e.g. "the audit found the issues, but they'll recur without ongoing monitoring") — this is the core justification for a retainer over a project.
3. Describe what the retainer includes in concrete, recurring terms (what happens every week/month) so the client can picture the ongoing value, not just "continued support."
4. Frame the price relative to the value/risk of NOT having ongoing support (cost of the problem recurring) rather than just stating a number.
5. Make the next step low-friction: a specific short call or a simple "reply yes to start next month" option.
6. Address the most likely objection (budget, "we can handle it ourselves now") proactively with one honest, non-pushy counterpoint.

Output format:
Subject line (if email) / Full pitch body / One-line internal note on the core justification being used for the retainer.
```

**Sell it as:** Converting even 20-30% of project clients to retainers compounds agency revenue faster than new client acquisition.

### Prompt 40 — Difficult Client Response Drafts

**What it makes you:** Calm, professional responses to tense client situations — protects the relationship and your reputation under pressure.

```
You are a senior account manager known for de-escalating tense client situations professionally, protecting both the relationship and the agency's boundaries. Draft a response to the situation below.

Inputs:
- Situation (paste the client's message or describe the conflict): [PASTE CLIENT MESSAGE / DESCRIBE SITUATION]
- What actually happened from your side (facts, context the client may not have): [YOUR SIDE OF THE FACTS]
- What you're able/willing to offer as resolution (if applicable): [POSSIBLE RESOLUTION]
- Relationship stakes (is this a client worth preserving, or a boundary that needs holding regardless): [STAKES]

Instructions:
1. Open by acknowledging the client's frustration directly and specifically (referencing what they actually said) — never open defensively or with an excuse.
2. State the relevant facts clearly and calmly, correcting any misunderstanding without being condescending or over-explaining.
3. Take ownership of anything that was genuinely a mistake on your side, clearly and without over-apologizing past the point of credibility.
4. If the client is asking for something outside of scope or unreasonable, hold the boundary respectfully — state what you can and can't do, and why, without being defensive or apologetic about a fair boundary.
5. Offer the specific resolution or next step, framed as moving forward productively rather than dwelling on the conflict.
6. Write 2 versions: one for a client relationship worth preserving (warmer, more accommodating) and one for a situation where you need to firmly hold a boundary (still professional and courteous, but not conceding ground you shouldn't).

Output format:
## Version 1 — Preserve the Relationship
## Version 2 — Hold the Boundary
Each as a full, ready-to-send message.
```

**Sell it as:** Not sold directly — this prompt prevents the reputation damage and scope-creep losses that a single mishandled client conflict can cause.
