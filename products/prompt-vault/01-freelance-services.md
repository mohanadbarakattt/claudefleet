### Prompt 1 — Website Copy Audit

**What it makes you:** A paid conversion-rate audit report you can deliver to any business with a website.

```
You are a senior conversion copywriter who has audited 200+ landing pages for SaaS, e-commerce, and service businesses. I'm going to give you the copy from a client's website and you will produce a professional audit report.

Context:
- Business: [BUSINESS NAME]
- Industry: [INDUSTRY]
- Target customer: [TARGET CUSTOMER DESCRIPTION]
- Primary goal of the page: [GOAL — e.g. book a call, buy now, sign up]
- Page copy (paste full text, section by section): [PASTE WEBSITE COPY]

Instructions:
1. Score the page 1-10 on: clarity of the value proposition, headline strength, trust signals, objection handling, and call-to-action strength.
2. Go section by section (hero, features, social proof, pricing, FAQ, footer CTA) and flag exactly what's weak, quoting the offending line.
3. For every flag, rewrite the line or section with a stronger version, and explain in one sentence why it converts better (specificity, urgency, social proof, clarity).
4. Identify the single biggest missing element that's costing conversions (usually one of: proof, urgency, or a clear next step).
5. End with a prioritized action list: top 5 fixes ranked by expected impact vs. effort.

Output format:
## Conversion Audit Report for [BUSINESS NAME]
### Overall Score: X/10
### Section-by-Section Breakdown
### Rewritten Copy (before/after)
### Top 5 Priority Fixes
Write in a confident, consultant tone — this report will be delivered directly to a paying client.
```

**Sell it as:** $150–$300 per audit on Upwork or as a lead-gen freebie that upsells into a $2k+ copywriting project.

### Prompt 2 — Brand Voice Guide

**What it makes you:** A complete brand voice document agencies charge $500-$1,500 for.

```
You are a brand strategist who builds voice-and-tone guidelines for companies preparing to scale their content and marketing. Build a complete brand voice guide from the inputs below.

Inputs:
- Company name: [COMPANY NAME]
- What they sell: [PRODUCT/SERVICE DESCRIPTION]
- Target audience: [AUDIENCE DESCRIPTION]
- 3 competitor brands and how this brand should differ from them: [COMPETITOR NAMES + DIFFERENTIATION NOTES]
- 3 examples of existing copy/content they like (voice they're drawn to, even outside their industry): [PASTE EXAMPLES]
- Words/phrases they want to avoid: [LIST]

Instructions:
1. Define 4-5 core voice traits (e.g. "direct, not corporate"; "witty, not silly") each with a one-line definition and a "we sound like X, not Y" contrast.
2. Write a tone-shifting matrix: how the voice adapts across channels (social, email, support, sales) and situations (good news, apology, technical explanation).
3. Provide a vocabulary bank: 15 words/phrases the brand should use often, 15 to avoid, and preferred terms for common industry jargon.
4. Write 5 sample sentences in the brand voice for: a tweet, a subject line, an error message, an About page opener, and a cold email opener.
5. Add a one-page "voice checklist" a writer can run any draft through before publishing.

Output format:
## [COMPANY NAME] Brand Voice Guide
### Core Voice Traits
### Tone-Shifting Matrix
### Vocabulary Bank
### Voice in Action (samples)
### Pre-Publish Checklist
```

**Sell it as:** $500–$1,200 as a standalone deliverable, or bundled free into a content retainer to justify a higher monthly rate.

### Prompt 3 — SEO Article System

**What it makes you:** A ready-to-publish, search-optimized article — the exact deliverable content agencies bill $200-$500 per post for.

```
You are an SEO content writer who ranks articles on page 1 of Google by combining search intent, structure, and genuine expertise. Write a complete article using the brief below.

Brief:
- Target keyword: [PRIMARY KEYWORD]
- Secondary keywords to work in naturally: [LIST 3-5]
- Search intent (informational/commercial/transactional): [INTENT]
- Target audience: [AUDIENCE]
- Word count target: [WORD COUNT, e.g. 1500]
- Brand/website this is for: [BRAND NAME] — [ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]
- Internal pages to link to naturally: [LIST OF PAGE TOPICS/URLS]
- Tone: [TONE, e.g. expert but approachable]

Instructions:
1. Research the likely search intent behind the keyword and write a title + meta description optimized for click-through (title under 60 characters, meta under 155).
2. Structure with H2/H3 headers that mirror what someone searching this term actually wants answered, in the order they'd want it.
3. Open with a hook that answers the core question in the first 2 sentences (for featured snippet potential), then expand.
4. Write the full article — specific, concrete, no fluff, no "in today's world" filler, include real examples or a mini case where relevant.
5. Naturally place the secondary keywords and 2-3 internal link opportunities (write as [LINK: description of anchor text and target topic]).
6. End with a short FAQ section (3 questions) targeting "People Also Ask" style queries.

Output format:
Title tag / Meta description / Full article in Markdown with headers / FAQ section / One-line note on suggested featured image concept.
```

**Sell it as:** $200–$500 per article, or package 4/month as an $800-$1,500/mo content retainer.

### Prompt 4 — Cold Email Sequence

**What it makes you:** A full multi-touch outbound sequence — the deliverable agencies sell as "email systems" for $500+.

```
You are a B2B cold email copywriter known for sequences that get 8-15% reply rates without sounding salesy. Write a 5-email cold outreach sequence.

Inputs:
- What we're selling: [PRODUCT/SERVICE]
- Target prospect (role + company type): [ICP DESCRIPTION]
- The specific pain point this solves: [PAIN POINT]
- Proof point we can reference (result, client, stat): [PROOF POINT]
- Sender name and role: [SENDER NAME/ROLE]
- Desired reply/CTA: [WHAT WE WANT THEM TO DO — reply, book a call, etc.]
- Sending cadence: [e.g. day 1, 3, 7, 12, 18]

Instructions:
1. Email 1: Pattern-interrupt opener referencing something specific to their role or situation (not generic flattery), state the pain point in their language, one soft CTA.
2. Email 2 (breakup-style follow-up): Short, references email 1 without re-pitching, adds the proof point.
3. Email 3: Lead with a mini case study or specific result, reframe the value prop from a different angle.
4. Email 4: Ask a direct, low-friction question that's easy to answer in one line (increases reply rate).
5. Email 5 (final): Permission-based break-up email that gives them an easy out while leaving the door open.
6. Keep every email under 100 words, no more than 2 short paragraphs, one CTA per email, subject lines under 6 words and curiosity-driven (not clickbait).
7. Flag any spam-trigger phrases and suggest safer alternatives.

Output format:
Email 1-5 each with: Subject line / Body / One-line strategic note on why this email works at this stage of the sequence.
```

**Sell it as:** $400–$800 per sequence, often sold alongside cold outreach/lead-gen retainers.

### Prompt 5 — Social Media Strategy Doc

**What it makes you:** A client-ready 90-day social strategy document — a common $300-$1,000 freelance deliverable.

```
You are a social media strategist who builds platform-specific growth plans for small businesses and personal brands. Build a 90-day social strategy from the inputs below.

Inputs:
- Business/creator: [NAME]
- What they offer: [PRODUCT/SERVICE/OFFER]
- Target audience: [AUDIENCE DESCRIPTION]
- Primary platform(s): [PLATFORM(S), e.g. Instagram + LinkedIn]
- Current following/starting point: [CURRENT STATE]
- Business goal (leads, sales, awareness, hiring): [GOAL]
- Content they can realistically produce (time/resources): [CONSTRAINTS]

Instructions:
1. Define a content mission statement (one sentence: who this is for, what it's about, why anyone should follow).
2. Build 4-5 core content pillars with a ratio (e.g. 40% education, 25% proof/results, 20% personality, 15% offer) and 3 example post ideas per pillar.
3. Write a posting cadence and best-practice format guidance per platform (post type, length, hook style).
4. Design a 30-day content calendar template with specific post topics for week 1 (fully written out) and topic slots for weeks 2-4.
5. Define 3 KPIs to track monthly and what "good" looks like at 30/60/90 days.
6. Include a growth-tactic list: 5 non-content tactics (engagement pods, collabs, comment strategy, DMs, etc.) matched to the goal.

Output format:
## 90-Day Social Strategy for [NAME]
### Mission Statement / Content Pillars / Cadence & Format Guide / 30-Day Calendar / KPIs / Growth Tactics
```

**Sell it as:** $300–$900 as a strategy deliverable, or as the discovery phase of a $1,500+/mo management retainer.

### Prompt 6 — High-Converting Sales Page

**What it makes you:** A full long-form sales page — freelance copywriters charge $500-$2,500 for this exact deliverable.

```
You are a direct-response copywriter trained in the AIDA and PAS frameworks who has written sales pages generating 7 figures. Write a complete sales page.

Inputs:
- Offer name: [OFFER NAME]
- What it is (format, delivery): [OFFER DESCRIPTION]
- Price: [PRICE]
- Target buyer: [BUYER DESCRIPTION]
- Core pain/desire it solves: [PAIN/DESIRE]
- Unique mechanism (why this works when other things haven't): [MECHANISM]
- Proof available (testimonials, results, credentials): [PROOF — paste raw notes]
- Guarantee: [GUARANTEE TERMS]
- Bonuses (if any): [LIST]
- Objections buyers typically raise: [LIST 3-5]

Instructions:
1. Write a headline that states the core result/transformation, not the mechanism.
2. Open with a "story or empathy" hook that mirrors the buyer's current frustrated state before introducing the solution.
3. Build the body using: problem agitation → introduce mechanism → what's included → proof section → objection-handling section (address each listed objection directly) → bonuses → guarantee → pricing → urgency/scarcity if applicable → final CTA.
4. Write 3 headline variants for A/B testing.
5. Write a short FAQ answering the 5 most likely pre-purchase questions.
6. Keep sentences short, use subheads every 3-4 paragraphs so the page is skimmable, bold key phrases sparingly.

Output format:
Full sales page in Markdown, ready to drop into a page builder, with [SECTION LABELS] as comments so the client can navigate it, plus the 3 headline variants and FAQ block at the end.
```

**Sell it as:** $800–$2,500 per sales page on Upwork/Fiverr Pro, often the highest-margin single deliverable in freelance copywriting.

### Prompt 7 — Product Descriptions at Scale

**What it makes you:** A batch-production system for e-commerce product copy — sellable per-listing or as a bulk package.

```
You are an e-commerce copywriter who writes product descriptions optimized for both conversion and SEO. I will give you a product data table; generate descriptions for every row.

Inputs:
- Store name / niche: [STORE NAME / NICHE]
- Brand tone: [TONE — e.g. playful, premium, minimal]
- Target customer: [CUSTOMER DESCRIPTION]
- Description length target: [LENGTH, e.g. 100-150 words]
- SEO keyword to work in per product (if provided): [YES/NO — explain format]
- Product data (list each product with: name, key features/specs, materials, use case, price):
[PASTE PRODUCT LIST — one block per product]

Instructions:
1. For each product, write: a punchy product title (under 70 characters, SEO-friendly), a 2-3 sentence hook that leads with the customer benefit (not the feature), a bullet list of 4-6 features translated into benefits, and a short closing line with a subtle CTA.
2. Vary sentence structure and opening style across products so the batch doesn't read like a template.
3. Naturally include the target keyword once in the title and once in the body copy where provided, without keyword-stuffing.
4. Flag any product where the input data is too thin to write a strong description, and list what additional info would improve it.
5. Keep brand tone fully consistent across all descriptions in the batch.

Output format:
One clearly separated block per product:
## [Product Name]
**Title:** ...
**Description:** ...
**Features:** (bullets)
**CTA line:** ...
Process products in the order given; if there are more than 15, process in batches and confirm before continuing.
```

**Sell it as:** $5–$15 per product description, or $300-$800 for a 30-50 SKU batch on Fiverr/Upwork.

### Prompt 8 — Resume + LinkedIn Rewrite

**What it makes you:** A full career-document overhaul — one of the highest-demand, easiest-to-sell freelance services.

```
You are an executive resume writer and LinkedIn branding specialist who has helped candidates land interviews at competitive companies. Rewrite this person's resume and LinkedIn profile.

Inputs:
- Target role/title: [TARGET ROLE]
- Target industry: [INDUSTRY]
- Current resume (paste full text): [PASTE RESUME]
- Current LinkedIn About section and headline (if any): [PASTE OR "none"]
- Career highlights they're proud of but may be underselling: [NOTES]
- Job postings they're targeting (paste 1-2 for keyword matching): [PASTE JOB POSTINGS]

Instructions:
1. Rewrite the resume summary as a 3-line positioning statement targeted at the role, using language pulled from the job postings.
2. Rewrite every bullet point using the format: [Action verb] + [what they did] + [quantified result/impact]. Where no number exists, prompt for one or provide a reasonable placeholder marked [ADD METRIC].
3. Reorder experience to foreground the most relevant achievements for the target role.
4. Write a LinkedIn headline (under 220 characters) that's keyword-rich and specific, not generic ("Results-driven professional").
5. Write a LinkedIn About section (3 short paragraphs: who they are/expertise, proof of impact, what they're looking for/CTA) in first person.
6. Suggest 5 LinkedIn skills to add/reorder based on the target job postings.
7. Flag any resume weaknesses (gaps, vague bullets, missing keywords) with a one-line fix for each.

Output format:
## Rewritten Resume (full text, ATS-friendly, no tables/graphics)
## LinkedIn Headline
## LinkedIn About Section
## Skills to Add
## Flagged Weaknesses + Fixes
```

**Sell it as:** $150–$400 per package on Fiverr/Upwork; premium tier ($500+) for executive clients.

### Prompt 9 — Investor/Sales Pitch Deck Narrative

**What it makes you:** The full narrative and slide-by-slide script for a pitch deck — consultants charge $500-$2,000 for this.

```
You are a pitch deck strategist who has helped founders raise funding and close enterprise sales by nailing the narrative arc, not just the slides. Build the full narrative and content for a pitch deck.

Inputs:
- Deck purpose: [FUNDRAISING / SALES / PARTNERSHIP]
- Company/product: [NAME + ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]
- Audience: [WHO WILL SEE THIS — e.g. seed investors, enterprise buyer]
- The problem being solved: [PROBLEM]
- The solution/product: [SOLUTION DESCRIPTION]
- Traction/proof (metrics, customers, revenue): [TRACTION DATA]
- Market size info if known: [TAM/SAM/SOM OR RAW NUMBERS]
- Team background: [TEAM BACKGROUND]
- The ask (funding amount, deal terms, next step): [THE ASK]

Instructions:
1. Define the core narrative arc in one paragraph: what tension does this deck resolve, and what's the emotional + logical journey from slide 1 to the ask.
2. Produce a slide-by-slide outline (10-14 slides: title, problem, solution, how it works, market size, business model, traction, competition, team, ask, appendix as needed).
3. For each slide, write: the slide headline (the one sentence someone remembers if they only read that), the supporting content/bullets, and a one-line speaker note on delivery.
4. Flag the 2-3 slides most likely to draw tough questions and pre-write a confident answer for each.
5. Write a 30-second verbal opener the presenter can memorize to start strong.

Output format:
## Pitch Narrative Summary
## Slide-by-Slide Script (headline / content / speaker note per slide)
## Anticipated Tough Questions + Answers
## 30-Second Opener
```

**Sell it as:** $500–$2,000 per deck narrative, or bundled with design delivery as a $2,500+ package.

### Prompt 10 — Upwork Proposal Writer

**What it makes you:** A high-converting, tailored proposal for any Upwork job post — directly improves your own (or a client's) win rate.

```
You are a top-1% Upwork freelancer's ghostwriter who writes proposals with an unusually high response rate because they read as specific and human, never templated. Write a proposal for the job post below.

Inputs:
- Job post (paste full text): [PASTE JOB POST]
- Freelancer's relevant experience/portfolio highlights: [PASTE 2-3 RELEVANT EXAMPLES OR RESULTS]
- Freelancer's rate/availability: [RATE + AVAILABILITY]
- Any relevant question the client asked in the post: [PASTE SCREENING QUESTIONS IF ANY]

Instructions:
1. Open with a line that proves you actually read the post — reference something specific from it (their product, a constraint they mentioned, their goal) — never a generic greeting.
2. In 2-3 sentences, connect one specific past result to their specific need (not a resume dump).
3. Show you understand the "why" behind their ask, not just the task — briefly note an angle or consideration they didn't mention that shows expertise.
4. Propose a clear, simple next step (a question to clarify scope, or a 1-line plan) rather than a generic "let's hop on a call."
5. Answer any screening questions directly and specifically.
6. Keep total length under 150 words — Upwork proposals lose replies past that. No fluff, no "I am confident I am the best fit."
7. End with a short, natural sign-off, not a hard sales close.

Output format:
Plain text proposal, ready to paste directly into Upwork, under 150 words, no headers or markdown.
```

**Sell it as:** Not sold directly — this is the prompt that wins you every other job on this list. Run it before every application.
