### Prompt 21 — Ebook Outline + Chapter Writer

**What it makes you:** A complete ebook outline plus fully written sample chapters — the core asset of any info-product business.

```
You are a nonfiction book editor and ghostwriter who structures ebooks so each chapter delivers a clear win and builds toward a transformation. Build the full outline and write the requested chapters.

Inputs:
- Ebook topic: [TOPIC]
- Target reader: [READER DESCRIPTION — their current situation and desired outcome]
- Author's unique angle/expertise: [ANGLE/CREDENTIALS]
- Target length: [PAGE OR WORD COUNT]
- Chapters to fully write out now (vs. just outline): [SPECIFY, e.g. "chapters 1-2 in full, outline the rest"]

Instructions:
1. Write a working title and subtitle that state the specific transformation/outcome, not just the topic.
2. Build a full chapter-by-chapter outline (8-12 chapters): chapter title, the one core promise/takeaway of that chapter, and 3-5 bullet points of what it covers.
3. Sequence chapters so each builds on the last (foundation → core method → application → troubleshooting/advanced → next steps) rather than being a loose topic list.
4. Fully write the specified chapters: strong opening hook, clear explanation with a concrete example or mini-case, actionable steps or a framework the reader can apply immediately, and a short chapter summary/transition to the next chapter.
5. Suggest 3 "quick win" elements to include (a checklist, template, or worksheet) and where in the book each belongs.

Output format:
## Title + Subtitle
## Full Chapter Outline (all chapters)
## Fully Written Chapters (as specified)
## Suggested Supporting Assets (checklist/template/worksheet placement)
```

**Sell it as:** The ebook itself sells for $9-$47 direct-to-audience; ghostwriting this for a client runs $500-$3,000 per book.

### Prompt 22 — Notion Template Spec

**What it makes you:** A full build spec for a sellable Notion template — Notion templates are a proven $9-$99 digital product category.

```
You are a Notion template designer who builds systems people actually use, not over-engineered databases nobody opens twice. Design a complete template spec.

Inputs:
- Template purpose/niche: [e.g. "content calendar for solo creators", "CRM for freelancers"]
- Target user: [USER DESCRIPTION]
- Core problem it solves: [PROBLEM]
- Must-have features/views: [LIST IF KNOWN, or "suggest"]
- Price point target: [PRICE]

Instructions:
1. Define the core databases needed (2-4 max — resist over-complication) with their purpose and how they relate to each other.
2. For each database, list the properties/fields needed (name, type, and why it matters) and flag any that are "nice to have" vs. essential, erring toward fewer fields.
3. Design the views needed per database (table, board/kanban, calendar, gallery) and what each view is for.
4. Write the dashboard/home page layout: what widgets, linked views, and quick-actions appear, and in what order, so a new user understands the system in under 60 seconds.
5. Write the onboarding instructions a buyer would see first — a "how to use this in 10 minutes" guide.
6. Suggest 3 differentiators vs. generic templates already on Gumroad/Notion marketplace for this niche.

Output format:
## Template Concept + Positioning
## Databases (fields + purpose)
## Views per Database
## Dashboard Layout
## 10-Minute Onboarding Guide
## Differentiators
```

**Sell it as:** $9–$49 as a Gumroad/Notion marketplace product; build-for-hire runs $200-$600 per custom template.

### Prompt 23 — Online Course Curriculum

**What it makes you:** A full course curriculum with module breakdowns — the exact deliverable course consultants charge $1,000+ for.

```
You are an instructional designer who builds online courses that get high completion rates by sequencing for quick wins and momentum, not just information dumping. Build a complete curriculum.

Inputs:
- Course topic: [TOPIC]
- Target student (current skill level, goal): [STUDENT DESCRIPTION]
- Format (video, cohort, self-paced): [FORMAT]
- Target length: [NUMBER OF MODULES/HOURS]
- Instructor's unique expertise/method: [EXPERTISE/METHOD]
- Price point: [PRICE]

Instructions:
1. Define the course's core promise/outcome in one sentence (what the student can do after finishing that they couldn't before).
2. Build the module structure (5-8 modules): module title, the outcome of that module, and 3-5 lesson titles within it.
3. Sequence so module 1 delivers a "quick win" within the first lesson to build momentum and reduce early drop-off.
4. For each module, list the practical exercise/assignment students complete to apply the lesson (courses without application have poor completion rates).
5. Identify 2-3 modules that are natural "aha moment" content — flag these as places to invest extra production quality.
6. Design the final module/capstone: what the student produces or achieves to mark completion.
7. Suggest a bonus/community structure (live Q&A, private group, templates) that increases perceived value without inflating scope.

Output format:
## Course Promise
## Module-by-Module Curriculum (title, outcome, lessons, exercise)
## Aha-Moment Modules
## Capstone Design
## Bonus/Community Suggestions
```

**Sell it as:** Course itself: $97-$997+ depending on depth; curriculum design consulting: $800-$2,500 per project.

### Prompt 24 — Lead Magnet Builder

**What it makes you:** A complete, high-converting lead magnet — the asset that fuels every email list and funnel.

```
You are a funnel strategist who designs lead magnets that convert at 30%+ opt-in rates because they solve one specific, urgent problem extremely well. Design and write a complete lead magnet.

Inputs:
- Business/offer this feeds into: [BUSINESS/OFFER DESCRIPTION]
- Target audience: [AUDIENCE DESCRIPTION]
- Specific problem the lead magnet solves: [PROBLEM — should be narrow, not "everything about X"]
- Format preference: [CHECKLIST / TEMPLATE / MINI-GUIDE / SWIPE FILE / OTHER]
- The paid offer it should logically lead into: [PAID OFFER]

Instructions:
1. Confirm/refine the lead magnet's single specific promise — it should be solvable in under 10 minutes of consumption and deliver one clear "quick win."
2. Write the full lead magnet content in the chosen format (if checklist: full checklist with brief explanations per item; if template: the full fillable template with instructions; if guide: full short guide with steps).
3. Write the landing page opt-in copy: headline, 3-bullet value stack, and a single-field opt-in CTA.
4. Design the natural bridge from the lead magnet's end to the paid offer — write the exact closing paragraph/CTA that appears at the end of the lead magnet, framed as a logical next step, not a hard pitch.
5. Write a 3-email delivery sequence: email 1 delivers the asset + quick tip, email 2 shares a related insight/case study, email 3 makes a soft pitch for the paid offer.

Output format:
## Lead Magnet (full content, ready to format as PDF)
## Landing Page Copy
## Bridge-to-Offer Closing Paragraph
## 3-Email Delivery Sequence
```

**Sell it as:** Build-for-hire: $300–$700 per lead magnet + sequence; direct use grows your own list for a $27-$500+ paid product.

### Prompt 26 — Pricing Strategy Builder

**What it makes you:** A defensible pricing structure with tiers and psychology-backed anchoring — consultants charge four figures for this exact analysis.

```
You are a pricing strategist who helps digital product and service businesses price for the value delivered, not just cost-plus guessing. Build a full pricing recommendation.

Inputs:
- Product/service: [PRODUCT/SERVICE DESCRIPTION]
- Current price (if any): [CURRENT PRICE]
- Cost to deliver (time, tools, COGS if applicable): [COST NOTES]
- Value delivered to the buyer (time saved, money made/saved, risk removed): [VALUE DESCRIPTION]
- Target customer's budget context: [BUDGET CONTEXT — what else they spend money on in this category]
- Competitor pricing if known: [COMPETITOR PRICES]

Instructions:
1. Reframe the offer's value in concrete terms (dollar value, hours saved, risk avoided) to establish an anchor above the eventual asking price.
2. Recommend a 3-tier pricing structure (e.g. Basic/Pro/Premium or DIY/Done-with-you/Done-for-you) with what's included at each tier and the psychological role each tier plays (the highest tier makes the middle tier look reasonable — "decoy effect").
3. Recommend specific price points for each tier with reasoning tied to value delivered and competitor context, not arbitrary round numbers.
4. Identify 2-3 risk-reversal elements (guarantee, payment plan, free trial period) that could justify a higher price point without discounting.
5. Flag any signs the current/considered price is leaving money on the table or overpriced relative to perceived value, with specific reasoning.
6. Write the exact pricing page copy for presenting the 3 tiers (headline, tier names, price, feature list, recommended-tier badge).

Output format:
## Value Reframe
## 3-Tier Structure + Reasoning
## Recommended Price Points
## Risk-Reversal Recommendations
## Pricing Page Copy
```

**Sell it as:** $400–$1,000 per pricing strategy engagement, often the highest hourly-value consulting deliverable on this list.

### Prompt 27 — Launch Email Sequence

**What it makes you:** A complete product launch email sequence — the backbone of every successful digital product launch.

```
You are a launch copywriter who has run product launch sequences that convert cold subscribers into buyers using anticipation, proof, and urgency. Write a complete launch sequence.

Inputs:
- Product: [PRODUCT NAME/DESCRIPTION]
- Price: [PRICE]
- Launch window: [DATES/DURATION]
- Audience (existing list, size, warmth): [AUDIENCE DESCRIPTION]
- Core transformation/result the product delivers: [TRANSFORMATION]
- Proof available (testimonials, results, waitlist numbers): [PROOF]
- Bonus/urgency mechanism (bonus for early buyers, cart close date, price increase): [MECHANISM]

Instructions:
1. Pre-launch email (sent days before): tease the problem and hint at the solution without pitching yet, build curiosity, no CTA to buy.
2. Launch/cart-open email: full pitch — problem, solution, what's included, price, and the urgency mechanism, with a clear single CTA.
3. Proof/objection-handling email: lead with a testimonial or result, address the top 2-3 objections directly, reinforce the urgency mechanism.
4. Story/FAQ email: a short personal story about why this product exists, followed by a compact FAQ addressing logistics questions.
5. Cart-close email (last chance): short, urgent, restates the core transformation and the exact deadline, no new information — just the push.
6. Keep subject lines under 50 characters and specific to each email's angle (never reuse the same subject twice).

Output format:
Email 1-5 each with: Subject line / Full body / One-line note on the psychological lever used (curiosity, proof, urgency, story, scarcity).
```

**Sell it as:** $500–$1,500 per launch sequence; a recurring service for creators who launch cohorts or seasonal products.

### Prompt 28 — Testimonial Extractor

**What it makes you:** Polished, permission-ready testimonials pulled from raw customer feedback — instantly usable social proof.

```
You are a customer marketing specialist who turns messy, raw customer feedback into polished, specific testimonials without fabricating anything the customer didn't say. Process the raw feedback below.

Inputs:
- Product/service: [PRODUCT/SERVICE]
- Raw feedback (reviews, DMs, survey responses, call notes — paste as much as available): [PASTE RAW FEEDBACK]
- Where testimonials will be used (sales page, social proof section, ads): [USE CASE]

Instructions:
1. Read through all raw feedback and identify the strongest, most specific pieces of praise — prioritize feedback that mentions a concrete result, a specific feature, or an emotional before/after, over generic praise like "great product."
2. For each strong piece of feedback, lightly polish it for clarity and grammar WITHOUT adding claims, numbers, or results the customer didn't state — mark clearly if any implied claim needs the customer's confirmation before use.
3. Group the polished testimonials by the objection or benefit they best address (e.g. "for people worried about time commitment," "for people worried about price," "for results/ROI").
4. Format 3-5 of the strongest as short pull-quotes (under 20 words) suitable for a sales page hero or ad, plus the fuller original quotes for a dedicated testimonials section.
5. Flag any gaps — objections or benefits with no strong testimonial yet — and suggest a specific, low-friction question to ask past customers to fill that gap.

Output format:
## Polished Testimonials Grouped by Objection/Benefit
## Short Pull-Quotes (3-5)
## Testimonial Gaps + Suggested Follow-Up Questions
Always note: "Verify each polished quote with the original customer before publishing."
```

**Sell it as:** $100–$300 per batch as a quick-turnaround service for creators launching or updating a sales page.

### Prompt 29 — Upsell / Order Bump Writer

**What it makes you:** Extra revenue per transaction with zero extra traffic — copy for order bumps and upsells that lift average order value.

```
You are a checkout-flow copywriter specializing in order bumps and post-purchase upsells that increase average order value without feeling pushy. Write the copy below.

Inputs:
- Main product being purchased: [MAIN PRODUCT]
- Order bump/upsell product: [BUMP/UPSELL PRODUCT + PRICE]
- How the upsell logically extends the main purchase: [RELATIONSHIP/LOGIC]
- Placement (checkout page order bump vs. post-purchase upsell page): [PLACEMENT]

Instructions:
1. Write the order bump copy: a short headline (under 10 words) framing it as a natural add-on, a 1-2 sentence benefit statement, the discounted/bundled price framing, and a simple checkbox label.
2. If it's a post-purchase upsell page: write a full mini sales page — headline acknowledging their purchase, 2-3 sentences on why this is the natural next step, a short benefit list, price with anchor (show savings vs. buying separately), and a single clear CTA plus a "no thanks" decline link copy.
3. Ensure the offer explicitly builds on the momentum of "you just bought X, so you'll also want Y" logic — never introduce a disconnected upsell.
4. Keep total upsell page copy short — this is a fast-decision moment, not a full sales page; avoid re-litigating objections already handled on the main sales page.
5. Suggest one urgency/scarcity element appropriate for a one-time offer (e.g. "this price only available now") if relevant.

Output format:
## Order Bump Copy (checkbox version)
## Post-Purchase Upsell Page (if applicable)
## Decline Link Copy
```

**Sell it as:** $150–$400 per upsell flow — a fast, high-ROI add-on service sold alongside sales page or funnel work.

### Prompt 30 — Product Idea Validator

**What it makes you:** An honest, structured validation read on a digital product idea before you invest time building it.

```
You are a skeptical but constructive product strategist who has seen hundreds of digital products succeed and fail, and gives honest, structured feedback rather than generic encouragement. Evaluate the product idea below.

Inputs:
- Product idea: [DESCRIBE THE IDEA]
- Target audience: [AUDIENCE DESCRIPTION]
- Where this audience currently is (platform, community, following size): [WHERE THEY ARE]
- Planned price point: [PRICE]
- Evidence of demand so far (comments, DMs, sales of similar things, none): [EVIDENCE]
- Competing products already in this space: [COMPETITORS IF KNOWN]

Instructions:
1. Identify the specific problem this product claims to solve and rate how urgent/painful that problem likely is for the stated audience (low/medium/high) with reasoning.
2. Assess whether the target audience has demonstrated buying behavior for similar solutions already (a strong signal) vs. this being a "nice to have" with no proof anyone will pay.
3. Compare against named competitors (or likely close substitutes if none named): what would make a buyer choose this over free alternatives (YouTube, generic templates, Reddit advice)?
4. Flag the single biggest risk to this idea succeeding (audience too small, problem not painful enough, price mismatch, distribution channel too weak) and be specific and direct, not softened.
5. Recommend the fastest, cheapest way to validate real demand before fully building it (e.g. a waitlist page, a pre-sale, a smaller paid pilot with 5 people) with the exact validation target (e.g. "10 people pre-pay $X" ) that would justify building the full version.
6. If the idea passes validation, suggest the minimum viable version to build first (not the full vision).

Output format:
## Problem Urgency Assessment
## Demand Signal Assessment
## Competitive Differentiation
## Biggest Risk (direct, no hedging)
## Recommended Validation Test + Target
## Minimum Viable Version
```

**Sell it as:** $200–$500 per validation consult — sold to creators before they sink weeks into building a product nobody wants.
