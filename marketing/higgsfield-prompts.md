# Higgsfield Prompts — one set per reel

> **Historical — these 24 prompts were rendered for the retired four-product
> catalog.** The clips in `generated/clips/` are real and the abstract footage
> (pipeline boards, hologram desks, macro shots) is still reusable, but the
> reel titles and CTA keywords here (STACK/VAULT/OS/FLEET) are dead.
> Current shooting script: **`production-scripts.md` (v3)** — two machines,
> keywords CONTENT / PRODUCT / BOTH / START.


Workflow: generate each scene as a 3–6s clip in Higgsfield, cut together in CapCut,
add the voiceover + on-screen text from reel-scripts.md. Keep one consistent
character across scenes (upload the same reference face/outfit image each time).
Aspect ratio: 9:16 for all. Style anchor to reuse in every prompt:
`cinematic, moody teal-and-orange grade, shallow depth of field, 35mm lens, soft practical lighting, photorealistic`

Camera: slow dolly-in.

**Scene 2:** Extreme close-up of a glowing terminal window, lines of text appearing rapidly like a job contract being signed, subtle screen glow reflecting on glasses, macro lens feel, cinematic teal-and-orange, photorealistic. Camera: static macro with focus pull.

**Scene 3:** Stylized visualization — five translucent holographic figures materializing one by one around a desk like employees clocking in, each a different color, dark office, volumetric light rays, cinematic sci-fi realism. Camera: slow orbital arc around the desk.

**Scene 4 (payoff):** The man asleep on a couch in the background, out of focus; in the foreground the monitor still working, code and documents flying across the screen, night ambiance, cinematic, warm practical lamp light vs cool screen light. Camera: locked-off wide, subtle timelapse feel.

## Consistency checklist
- Same character reference image in every scene generation.
- Same grade phrase (`teal-and-orange`) in every prompt — makes cuts feel like one film.
- Generate 2 takes per scene, keep the one with cleaner hands/faces.
- Hook scenes: always add a camera move toward the subject (push/punch-in) — movement in the first frame stops the scroll.
