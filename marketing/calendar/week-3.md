# ClaudeFleet — 30-Day Content Calendar — Week 3 (Days 15–21): Escalate

Per content-system.md's Week 3 arc: POV/skit (Skeptic, now voice-only) and guru-guide (Ava) content,
introducing the Digital Product Machine (PRODUCT, $99) mid-week and building toward
the both-machines close (BOTH, $149) by week's end. Cast, compliance rules, and CTA
keyword map live in content-system.md — this file follows them rather than repeating
them.

**Hook rule for this week:** every spoken hook must land inside three seconds. If it
has two clauses and a dash, it's a caption, not a hook. Beats carry the explanation.
(Story-sticker polls are exempt — a poll has to name both options to be a poll.)

**Faceless — see the block at the top of week-1.md before shooting.** Nobody appears
in frame. Every day is tagged with its shot mode: (a) screen-only + voiceover,
(b) Ava, or (c) text-on-screen with no voice. The **Skeptic** is still a played
character but is now **voice-only, off-screen, with hands-only inserts** — a hand on
a phone, a hand pushing a laptop lid — never a person in shot. The dramatization
labelling stays exactly as it was: an off-screen voice is still an acted voice, and
the "acted — real command, real output" card still has to appear. This week's
escalation is the week most tempted to reintroduce a presenter for authority; the
answer is a longer look at the real output instead.

## Day 15 — POV/SKIT
**Shot mode:** (a) Screen-only + voiceover. Two voices over one unbroken screen recording: the Skeptic (played, off-screen) and the ClaudeFleet voice. Hands-only inserts allowed. Nobody in frame.
**Hook:** "POV: I asked it to validate my idea. It told me not to build it." *(on-screen "POV:" convention — this is an acted bit, and the audience must be able to tell)*
**Beats:**
1. Skeptic (played character, **voice only, off-screen**), flat: "every tool that offers to 'validate your idea' says yes. That's how it sells you the build."
2. Screen fills the frame: `/dp-validate` runs live on a real, unremarkable idea. Unedited — the output comes back genuinely lukewarm on demand signal.
3. Skeptic, off-screen, mid-scroll: "wait. It just told you *no*." Hands-only insert if you want the beat: a hand stopping the trackpad scroll, cropped at the wrist.
4. ClaudeFleet voiceover, plain: "No demand signal, no build. That's stage one, and it's a gate — not a suggestion." Cursor scrolls back up to the actual line in the memo that says so; hold on it long enough to read.
5. Skeptic, off-screen, quieter: "…that's the first one that's ever cost me nothing to find out."
6. On-screen text card, held: "acted voices — real command, real output."
**Visual note:** Screen-record a real Claude session running `/dp-validate` on an actually mediocre idea, showing a genuinely unconvinced assessment — no fabricated demand scores, no cherry-picked "amazing idea!" output. The recording runs full-frame for the whole piece; the skit lives entirely in the audio and in caption cards. Skeptic is a **voice performance only** — cast for voice, record it dry and close-mic'd so it reads as a real reaction rather than a polished VO, per market-research.md's "UGC outperforms polished ads" finding. Hands-only inserts must be cropped so no person is identifiable. Production note: this is still a character bit even without anyone in shot — the "acted voices" card is mandatory, and never caption it as a real customer review.
**Caption:** Quick bit — the sceptic is a played voice, the command is not. The line is that every "validate your idea" feature just says yes to close the sale, so I ran the real `/dp-validate` on a mid idea and left the actual output on screen, unedited, for you to read yourself. ## Day 18 — CAROUSEL
**Shot mode:** (c) Text-on-screen, no voice. Static slides carrying real screenshots.
**Hook (slide 1):** "The stage everyone skips is slide 2."
**Beats (one slide per stage — 7 slides total):**
1. **Slide 1 — cover:** "The stage everyone skips is slide 2." Clean, high contrast, readable at thumbnail size — the cover withholds which stage it is, so the swipe is the payoff.
2. **Slide 2 — VALIDATE:** what goes in (an idea), what comes out (a memo with real demand signals and comparable paid products), and the gate: no GO, no build.
3. **Slide 3 — BUILD:** the validated problem statement becomes the actual deliverable. Every section has to trace back to the memo.
4. **Slide 4 — PACKAGE:** manifest, buyer README, terms, cover-art briefs. The step that makes it sellable rather than just written.
5. **Slide 5 — LAUNCH KIT:** sales page, five-email sequence, launch-week posts — written from the validation memo, not from imagination.
6. **Slide 6 — SCHEDULED:** the plan goes to Postiz. It stops there and waits for you to approve. Nothing publishes itself.
7. **Slide 7 — close:** "Runs on your own Claude, Higgsfield and Postiz. $99. Comment PRODUCT."
**Visual note:** Static carousel, no video. Dark background matching the brand, one idea per slide, large type readable at thumbnail size. Slides 2–6 each carry a small real screenshot of that stage's actual output file as the visual — real artifacts, not mockups or icons. Carousels earn saves, which is the metric this format is here for.
**Caption:** The whole Digital Product Machine, one slide per stage — validate, build, package, launch kit, scheduled. Slide 2 is the one most people skip and it's the only one that stops you building something nobody asked for. Save it for when you're staring at an idea and can't tell if it's real. Comment PRODUCT for the full thing.

## Week 3 format mix (why these seven)
POV/SKIT · GURU-GUIDE ×2 · SCREEN-RECORD PROOF · CAROUSEL · ENGAGEMENT-BAIT STORY ·
CHEAT-CODE — six distinct pillars across seven days, with **two screen-record proof
days** (17 and 21) rather than one. Day 17 is unstylised end to end; Day 21 allows one
AI-generated cutaway (empty room, no people) on the hook line before cutting to the real recording. The carousel is here to earn saves, which is the
one metric this week's other formats don't chase. Days 15–18 carry PRODUCT to
introduce the second machine; 19–21 hand over to BOTH for the close.
