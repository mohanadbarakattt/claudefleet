# ClaudeFleet — 30-Day Content Calendar — Week 3 (Days 15–21): Escalate

Per content-system.md's Week 3 arc: POV/skit (Skeptic) and guru-guide (Ava) content,
introducing the Digital Product Machine (PRODUCT, $99) mid-week and building toward
the both-machines close (BOTH, $149) by week's end. Cast, compliance rules, and CTA
keyword map live in content-system.md — this file follows them rather than repeating
them.

**Hook rule for this week:** every spoken hook must land inside three seconds. If it
has two clauses and a dash, it's a caption, not a hook. Beats carry the explanation.
(Story-sticker polls are exempt — a poll has to name both options to be a poll.)

## Day 15 — POV/SKIT
**Hook:** "POV: I asked it to validate my idea. It told me not to build it." *(on-screen "POV:" convention — this is an acted bit, and the audience must be able to tell)*
**Beats:**
1. Skeptic (played character), flat: "every tool that offers to 'validate your idea' says yes. That's how it sells you the build."
2. Operator runs `/dp-validate` live on a real, unremarkable idea. Screen-record, unedited — the output comes back genuinely lukewarm on demand signal.
3. Skeptic leans in: "wait. It just told you *no*."
4. Operator, plain: "No demand signal, no build. That's stage one, and it's a gate — not a suggestion."
5. Skeptic, quieter: "…that's the first one that's ever cost me nothing to find out."
6. On-screen text card, held: "acted — real command, real output."
**Visual note:** Screen-record a real Claude session running `/dp-validate` on an actually mediocre idea, showing a genuinely unconvinced assessment — no fabricated demand scores, no cherry-picked "amazing idea!" output. Cut to Skeptic reaction shots handheld, natural light, phone-video UGC style — deliberately *not* the cinematic Higgsfield look, per market-research.md's "UGC outperforms polished ads" finding. Production note: this is a character bit — never caption it as a real customer review.
**Caption:** Quick bit — the sceptic is played, the command is not. He reckons every "validate your idea" feature just says yes to close the sale, so I ran the real `/dp-validate` on a mid idea and let the actual output land on camera. It came back unconvinced, so nothing got built. That is the entire point of putting that stage first. Comment PRODUCT and I'll walk you through the gate.

## Day 18 — CAROUSEL
**Hook (slide 1):** "The stage everyone skips is slide 2."
**Beats (one slide per stage — 7 slides total):**
1. **Slide 1 — cover:** "The stage everyone skips is slide 2." Clean, high contrast, readable at thumbnail size — the cover withholds which stage it is, so the swipe is the payoff.
2. **Slide 2 — VALIDATE:** what goes in (an idea), what comes out (a memo with real demand signals and comparable paid products), and the gate: no GO, no build.
3. **Slide 3 — BUILD:** the validated problem statement becomes the actual deliverable. Every section has to trace back to the memo.
4. **Slide 4 — PACKAGE:** manifest, buyer README, terms, cover-art briefs. The step that makes it sellable rather than just written.
5. **Slide 5 — LAUNCH KIT:** sales page, five-email sequence, launch-week posts — written from the validation memo, not from imagination.
6. **Slide 6 — SCHEDULED:** the plan goes to Postiz. It stops there and waits for you to approve. Nothing publishes itself.
7. **Slide 7 — close:** "Runs on your own Claude, Higgsfield and Postiz. $99. Comment PRODUCT."
**Visual note:** Static carousel, no video. Dark background matching the brand, one idea per slide, large type readable at thumbnail size. Slides 2–6 each carry a small real screenshot of that stage's actual output file as the visual — real artifacts, not mockups or icons. Carousels earn saves, which is the metric this format is here for.
**Caption:** The whole Digital Product Machine, one slide per stage — validate, build, package, launch kit, scheduled. Slide 2 is the one most people skip and it's the only one that stops you building something nobody asked for. Save it for when you're staring at an idea and can't tell if it's real. Comment PRODUCT for the full thing.
**CTA keyword:** PRODUCT

## Week 3 format mix (why these seven)
POV/SKIT · GURU-GUIDE ×2 · SCREEN-RECORD PROOF · CAROUSEL · ENGAGEMENT-BAIT STORY ·
CHEAT-CODE — six distinct pillars across seven days, with **two screen-record proof
days** (17 and 21) rather than one. Day 17 is unstylised end to end; Day 21 allows one
AI-generated cutaway on the hook line before cutting to the real recording. The carousel is here to earn saves, which is the
one metric this week's other formats don't chase. Days 15–18 carry PRODUCT to
introduce the second machine; 19–21 hand over to BOTH for the close.
